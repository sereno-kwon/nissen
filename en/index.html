01

Brand

NISSEN日泉ケーブル · Kishiwada, Osaka

The feel of your brakes
begins with a strand
you never see.

NISSEN (日泉ケーブル) is a bicycle cable maker in Kishiwada, Osaka, Japan. It opened in 1948 making springs for weaving looms, later began making bicycle brake wire, and took its current name, Nissen Cable, in 1973. For many years it has made OEM cables for bicycle makers, brake makers and wholesalers.

Nissen processes and sells bicycle brake outer casing and inner wire in Japan: stainless cables, vintage cables reissued from 1950s to 1970s Europe, and cables in special lengths for bikes such as tandems. A brand that has spent decades refining what you feel at your fingertips when you pull a lever.

Quality

Checked by hand, one by one.

Before shipping, Nissen tensile-tests every lot, and when packing, staff check each cable by eye and by touch for scratches or dirt. Cables with leftover oil, any kink at all, or frayed ends are all rejected. With inspection this strict, around 1 to 2% of every lot cannot be shipped.

The nipple (taiko) at the end of the inner cable is made to a tensile standard of at least 240 kgf, stricter than the 150 kgf of the Japanese JIS standard. However strong a cable is, it is dangerous if the end is poorly made.

StainlessRust resistant

Rust-resistant stainless instead of steel. It keeps its original smoothness through rain and sweat.

Structure19 to 31 strands

A standard inner cable has 19 strands. SP31 tightly twists 31 strands of different thicknesses, so it is more flexible and stretches less.

InspectionBy eye and by hand

Every cable is checked by eye and by hand. Only those that pass are packed and shipped.

02

Inner Cable

SP31Special Stainless Inner

31 strands, not 19,
smooth all the way to the lever.

A standard inner cable is twisted from 19 strands. SP31 has 31. Using strands of different thicknesses inside makes it more flexible, and the tight twist leaves fewer gaps between strands. That makes it easier to install through tight routing around the bars, and with less stretch there is less re-adjustment after fitting. Breaking strength is also about 10% higher.

The surface is shaved close to a perfect circle with a slick finish, then given a special electrodeposition coating. Friction is lower than with a slick finish alone or a PTFE (Teflon) coating, and the coating resists peeling, so the light pull lasts. It is also pre-stretched to reduce initial stretch.

01

Flexibility

The 31-strand construction bends more smoothly than conventional cable. Installation is easier even with today's tight routing around the bars, and operation feels lighter.

02

Durability

The tight twist means less stretch and greater load strength. About 10% higher breaking strength, and pre-stretching means less re-adjustment after fitting.

03

Low friction

Slick finish followed by electrodeposition coating. Lower friction than slick-only or PTFE-coated cables, with a coating that lasts.

N mark

Look for the N mark
on the nipple

SP31 needs dies and special plating that are hard to produce in Japan, so Nissen made dedicated dies (with the N mark) in Taiwan and produces it there to its own specification. All Nissen products other than SP31 are made in Japan.

Every lot is tensile-tested when it ships from Taiwan and again when it arrives in Japan, and any lot that falls short is returned or scrapped. Then staff inspect each cable by eye and by hand before packing. Genuine SP31 has an N mark on the nipple at the end of the cable.

03

Outer Casing

Stainless Outer27 Colors

A coil that won't rust,
the final line
that completes your bike.

Nissen replaces the steel coil normally used in outer casing with stainless steel. It resists rust, and the metal coil shows through a clear colour coating. A polyethylene liner inside lightens the brake lever pull. One outer lets you enjoy dressing up and upgrading at the same time.

The brake outer has a 5 mm outer diameter for 1.5 or 1.6 mm inner cable; the shift outer is 4 mm for 1.2 mm inner. Sold in 2 m lengths, enough for one road bike, in 27 colours from Clear and Clear Black to traditional Japanese colours.

High Performance

Stiffer,
lighter

Direct (brake) uses thicker coil wire with tighter gaps, greatly reducing compression under load. It keeps the flexibility while raising stiffness and giving a more direct braking feel. Recommended for mechanical disc brakes.

Premium (brake) adds a PTFE liner, also used in medical and semiconductor equipment, for lower friction and better durability. Sold only as a brake set with SP31 inner cables. Shift Ver.3 uses a thicker coil to handle the fine adjustment of 11-speed shifting.

Vintage Outer

Italian · FrenchReissued 1950 ~ 1970

Making the cables of the 1950s
again, today.

A reissue of the steel round-wire outer casing made in Europe from about 1950 to 1970. With a liner that the originals never had, the lever pulls lightly. Vintage in looks, modern in performance.

The colours were matched again and again to come as close as possible to the outers of the time: Black, White, Silver, Red, Blue, Yellow, French Blue, Celeste, British Green, Legnano Green, Sakura, Wakakusa and Indigo Blue, 13 in all. For brakes, sold in 2 m lengths.

for your bike.

Brake and shift cables differ in diameter and construction, and the basic rule is to choose an outer that matches your inner. Nissen itself recommends consulting a mechanic who can see your actual bike about installation and compatibility. Story

1948 ~ 2026Kishiwada, Osaka

From loom springs
to bicycle cables:
the Nissen story.

In 1948 a small factory in Kishiwada, Osaka was making springs for weaving looms. When it began making bicycle brake wire, it became Nissen Cable in 1973. For years it made cables behind the names of bicycle and brake makers; today it presents SP31, its colour outers and its vintage outers under the Nissen name.
